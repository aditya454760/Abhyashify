How the timetable is built

Subjects are spread across the days up to the exam date, weighted by importance. Your hours, times of day and days off are respected, a revision buffer and mock tests are kept at the end, and every block can be edited.

How to split the months you have left for UPSC

Prepkram divides the days to the exam into three phases: learn the syllabus, revise and practise, then a final stretch with weekly mock tests. Questions and answers

Which UPSC papers does Prepkram cover?

It covers: Civil Services (IAS): Prelims + Mains, Civil Services (IAS): Prelims only, Engineering Services (ESE / IES) Stage 1. Each one has its subjects preloaded, and you can change them.

When is UPSC held?

Prelims is usually late May or June, Mains about three months later. Usually late May or June. This is only a hint. Prepkram never treats it as the real date and asks you to confirm your own exam date against the official notice.

Can I change the UPSC subjects?

Yes. You can add, remove or rename subjects, and the Other option lets you build an exam that is not in the list. If a paper has optional subjects, the setup asks which one is yours.
