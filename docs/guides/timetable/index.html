How to make a study timetable for a competitive exam

A good exam timetable works backwards from your exam date: fix the date, count the days, split them into phases, give each subject a share by its importance and your confidence, and protect days for revision and mock tests. Here are the steps, with the split Prepkram uses.

How it works

  1. Fix the exam date and count the days Take the date from the exam body's official notice. Count the days from today to that date and subtract your days off.
  2. List every subject and give it two scores Rate each subject for importance (1 to 5) and for how confident you are (1 to 5). High importance with low confidence should get the most time.
  3. Split the days into three phases First learn the syllabus, then revise and practise, and finish with a final stretch of weekly mock tests and fixing the weak spots they show. The table below is the split Prepkram uses.
  4. Set your daily hours Use different hours for weekdays and days off, and pick the part of the day when you concentrate best.
  5. Spread the subjects by their scores Share each phase's days and daily hours across the subjects in proportion to their scores, and put the high-importance ones first.
  6. Give one day a week to a full mock test Pick a fixed day (usually the last study day of the week), sit a full-length mock, and review the mistakes the next day.
  7. Compare plan and reality every week Check the hours you planned against the hours you really studied. If the gap is large, change next week's plan instead of quietly piling up missed hours.

Questions and answers

What is the easiest way to make a study timetable?

Work backwards from the exam date: count the days, split them into learning, revision and a final stretch, then share each phase across your subjects in proportion to their importance and your confidence. Prepkram does all of this for you after asking for your exam, date and hours.

How many months before the exam should I start?

There is no single right answer. It depends on the exam and where you are now. The table above shows how Prepkram splits the time when 1 to 12 months are left.

Do I have to study every day?

In Prepkram you choose your days off and the timetable keeps those days free of blocks. How many days off suit you is your own call.

How do I know if I am on track?

Log the time you spend on each block. The weekly report shows planned hours next to the hours you actually studied.
